WINLINK PROPAGATION ADVISOR
User Guide and Explanation

Author: Peter Busha — WA1PB

============================================================
PURPOSE AND DISCLAIMER
============================================================

The Winlink Propagation Advisor is a personal project created by
Peter Busha (WA1PB) for fun, experimentation, education, and
amateur-radio use.

This software is provided as an experimental propagation-assistance
tool. It is not an official Winlink product and is not affiliated
with or endorsed by the Winlink Development Team, ARDC, PSKReporter,
or any particular Winlink gateway operator.

The information presented by the Advisor is intended to help an
operator understand current radio propagation conditions and identify
Winlink gateways that may be worth trying.

The Advisor does NOT guarantee that a connection will be successful.

Propagation can change rapidly. A gateway that appears to be an
excellent choice may not answer, may be busy, may be offline, or may
not be reachable when an actual connection is attempted.

The operator is responsible for selecting frequencies, operating the
radio, observing amateur-radio regulations, and deciding whether to
attempt a connection.

Use the information provided by this Advisor as guidance, not as a
guarantee.

============================================================
WHAT THE ADVISOR DOES
============================================================

The Winlink Propagation Advisor combines two types of information:

1. WSPR reception reports
2. Winlink gateway information

WSPR reports provide evidence of where signals from this station are
currently being heard.

Winlink gateway information identifies stations that may be available
for Winlink connections on various bands and modes.

The Advisor compares these two sets of information and attempts to
identify Winlink gateways whose locations and operating modes are
compatible with the propagation being observed.

The goal is simple:

    "Where is my signal currently being heard, and which Winlink
     gateways are located in areas where propagation appears useful?"

The Advisor is therefore a propagation-assistance tool, not a
connection controller.

============================================================
BAND SELECTION
============================================================

The Advisor can examine individual amateur-radio bands from:

    160m
    80m
    40m
    30m
    20m
    17m
    15m
    12m
    10m

The BAND selector determines which WSPR propagation information is
used when evaluating Winlink gateways.

AUTO mode allows the Advisor to determine the most appropriate current
WSPR band based on the most recent available WSPR reports.

Because WSPR is normally transmitted on one band at a time, using the
most recent band information can be more useful than combining old
reports from several different bands.

============================================================
WINLINK MODE
============================================================

The MODE selector allows the results to be limited to:

    VARA
    ARDOP
    BOTH

VARA displays Winlink gateways using the VARA HF protocol.

ARDOP displays Winlink gateways using the ARDOP protocol.

BOTH includes both types.

============================================================
WSPR PROPAGATION FOOTPRINT
============================================================

The WSPR Propagation Footprint summarizes where this station is
currently being heard.

The footprint is divided into geographic regions.

Examples include:

    Canada / North
    Mountain / Southwest
    West Coast
    Northeast / Mid-Atlantic
    Midwest
    Central Plains
    Southeast
    Europe / Atlantic
    Europe / Africa
    Other

The regional cards can be clicked.

Clicking a region filters the Winlink gateway list so that only
gateways located in that region are displayed.

The selected region remains highlighted while the other regions fade
into the background.

Clicking the selected region again returns to the overall gateway
ranking.

============================================================
WHAT IS A WSPR RECEIVER?
============================================================

A "receiver" in the Advisor is a unique WSPR receiving station that
reported hearing this station's WSPR transmission.

For example, if ten different amateur-radio stations report hearing
WA1PB, the Advisor may show:

    Receivers: 10

This does NOT mean that ten Winlink gateways are available.

It means that ten different WSPR receiving stations provided evidence
about current propagation.

Multiple WSPR reports from the same receiving station do not count as
multiple receivers.

The number of receivers is therefore an indication of how broadly
the signal is being heard.

Generally:

    More receivers = more propagation evidence

However, more receivers does not automatically mean that every
location between the transmitter and receiver will have a usable
Winlink path.

============================================================
FRESH REPORTS
============================================================

WSPR reports have an age.

A recent report is more useful for estimating current propagation than
an older report.

The Advisor therefore gives greater importance to recent reports.

A "fresh" report is a recently received WSPR report within the
Advisor's current freshness window.

Propagation can change quickly, so fresh reports are generally more
important than older reports.

============================================================
BEST AND AVERAGE SIGNAL
============================================================

The propagation information may include:

    Best
    Average

BEST represents the strongest useful WSPR signal observed in the
available evidence for the area or gateway.

AVERAGE represents the average signal level of the supporting WSPR
evidence.

Signal strength is expressed in dB.

A less-negative value generally represents a stronger received signal.

For example:

    -5 dB is stronger than -15 dB.

These values are observations of WSPR reception and should not be
interpreted as a guarantee of Winlink connection quality.

============================================================
GATEWAY CARDS
============================================================

Each Winlink gateway recommendation is presented as a gateway card.

A typical card contains information such as:

    Rank
    Callsign
    Grid
    Frequency
    Band
    Mode
    Score
    Receivers
    Signal information
    Other propagation information

The CALLSIGN identifies the Winlink gateway.

The GRID identifies the gateway's Maidenhead grid location.

The FREQUENCY is the frequency associated with the Winlink channel.

The BAND identifies the amateur-radio band.

The MODE identifies the Winlink protocol, such as VARA or ARDOP.

============================================================
RANKING
============================================================

The Advisor assigns each gateway a propagation score.

The score is intended to help sort gateways from more promising to
less promising choices based on the available propagation evidence.

The score considers several factors, including:

    - Current WSPR reception
    - Number of unique WSPR receivers
    - Signal strength
    - Age of the WSPR reports
    - Band compatibility
    - Geographic evidence
    - Distance to the Winlink gateway

The score is a relative ranking tool.

It should NOT be interpreted as:

    "A score of 40 guarantees a connection."

Instead, it means approximately:

    "Based on the information currently available, this gateway
     appears more promising than gateways with lower scores."

============================================================
DISTANCE
============================================================

Gateway distance is calculated geographically from the station grid
to the gateway grid.

Distance is displayed in kilometers.

Distance is only one part of the Advisor's evaluation.

A closer gateway is NOT necessarily a better gateway.

HF propagation can produce skip zones, long-distance paths, and other
conditions where a more distant gateway may actually provide a better
path.

For this reason, the Advisor does not simply rank gateways by
distance.

============================================================
REGIONAL RANKING
============================================================

The normal gateway list shows the overall ranking.

When a region is selected from the WSPR Propagation Footprint, the
gateway list is filtered to that region.

The gateways in the selected region are then displayed as a regional
Top 10.

For example, a gateway that is ranked #45 in the overall list might
become:

    #1

when its region is selected.

This does NOT mean its propagation score changed.

It means that it is currently the highest-ranked gateway among the
gateways in that selected region.

============================================================
DATA AGE
============================================================

The Advisor displays when its current result was generated.

For example:

    Updated just now

or:

    Updated 5 minutes ago

This describes the age of the Advisor's current result, not the age
of every individual WSPR report.

Individual WSPR reports can have different ages.

Always consider the age of the underlying propagation evidence when
evaluating a recommendation.

============================================================
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
============================================================

The Advisor cannot predict HF propagation with certainty.

WSPR reception is useful evidence, but WSPR and Winlink are different
applications with different operating characteristics.

A WSPR report proves that a signal was received somewhere.

It does not prove that a reliable two-way Winlink connection will
work between two particular stations.

Other factors can affect a Winlink connection, including:

    - Changing propagation
    - Solar and ionospheric conditions
    - Noise
    - Local interference
    - Antenna characteristics
    - Transmitter power
    - Receiver performance
    - Gateway availability
    - Gateway busy status
    - Frequency occupancy
    - Equipment configuration
    - Operator conditions

The Advisor should therefore be treated as a decision-support tool.

============================================================
PAT AND WINLINK
============================================================

The Advisor can help an operator identify a promising Winlink
gateway, but it does not replace PAT.

The normal operating workflow is:

    1. Review the Advisor.
    2. Select a promising band, region, and gateway.
    3. Open PAT.
    4. Review the connection information.
    5. Attempt the Winlink connection using the operator's normal
       procedure.

The Advisor is intended to help answer:

    "Which gateway should I try?"

It is not intended to automatically make that decision for the
operator.

============================================================
WHY USE WSPR?
============================================================

WSPR is useful because many amateur-radio stations continuously
monitor WSPR transmissions and report what they hear.

This creates a large distributed network of receiving stations.

The Advisor uses those reports as real-world evidence of where this
station's signal is currently propagating.

Instead of relying only on theoretical propagation predictions, the
Advisor asks:

    "Where is my actual signal being heard right now?"

That information can then be compared with the locations of Winlink
gateways.
